Approach overview

Evidence-linked qualitative concept analysis

A researcher-governed way to turn qualitative interview transcripts into reviewable concept records without separating an interpretation from the words that support it.

View the 62-rule operational checklist →

01

What method is this?

Evidence-linked qualitative concept analysis is the name used here for a structured approach to analyzing qualitative interview transcripts. A researcher separates transcript language into distinct participant concepts, writes a concise participant-centered summary for each, and preserves the quotation and reasoning behind every interpretation.

02

What do people get out of it?

The result is a reviewable collection of concept records. Each record connects an exact transcript quotation to one concept summary, its analytical reasoning, and its review status. The records can later be compared and grouped across participants without losing the original evidence.

03

What role does George Jensen play?

George Jensen designed and maintains the operational workflow and its supporting tools. Depending on the project, he may conduct the analysis, facilitate review, or act as a reviewer. AI can propose records and reasoning, but named researchers decide what is accepted.

04

Who created it?

This checklist sits within a broader family of qualitative practices concerned with participant language, meaning units, action-oriented coding, reflexive interpretation, and evidence-linked analysis. Its current formulation was created by George Jensen and was informed especially by his study of Indi Young’s methods. It does not represent her method or imply her endorsement.
