Approach overview

Evidence-linked qualitative concept analysis

A researcher-governed way to configure evidence-linked concept proposals from qualitative interview transcripts without separating an interpretation from the words that support it.

View the 62-rule agent manifest →

01

What method is this?

Evidence-linked qualitative concept analysis is the name used here for a structured approach to analyzing qualitative interview transcripts. The configured agent proposes separations of transcript language into distinct participant concepts, drafts a concise participant-centered summary for each, and preserves the quotation and reasoning behind every interpretation.

02

What do people get out of it?

The result is a reviewable collection of evidence-linked concept proposals. Each proposal connects an exact transcript quotation to one proposed summary, its analytical reasoning, and its review status. Accepted records can later be compared and grouped across participants without losing the original evidence.

03

What role does George Jensen play?

George Jensen authored and maintains the agent manifest configuration, operational workflow, and supporting tools. Depending on the project, he may facilitate review or act as a reviewer. The configured agent produces records and reasoning; named researchers review the proposals and decide what is accepted.

04

Who created it?

This manifest configuration sits within a broader family of qualitative practices concerned with participant language, meaning units, action-oriented coding, reflexive interpretation, and evidence-linked analysis. The manifest’s current formulation was created by George Jensen and was informed especially by his study of Indi Young’s methods. It does not represent her method or imply her endorsement.
